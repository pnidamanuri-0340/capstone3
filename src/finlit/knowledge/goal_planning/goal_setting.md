# Financial Goal Setting and Planning

## SMART goals
Effective financial goals are SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. "Save more" is vague; "Save $12,000 for a house down payment in 24 months by setting aside $500 per month" is SMART.

## Order of operations for savings
A common priority order is: (1) build a starter emergency fund of about $1,000 to $2,000; (2) contribute enough to a workplace retirement plan to capture any employer match; (3) pay off high-interest debt; (4) build a full three-to-six-month emergency fund; (5) increase retirement savings toward 15% of income; (6) save for other goals such as a home or education. Order can vary with circumstances.

## Short, medium and long-term goals
Short-term goals (under about 3 years) such as an emergency fund or vacation belong in safe, liquid places like high-yield savings or CDs. Medium-term goals (3 to 10 years) such as a home down payment may use a conservative mix. Long-term goals (over 10 years) such as retirement can accept more stock exposure because there is time to recover from downturns.

## Required monthly savings
To reach a target amount in a fixed time with a given return, the required monthly contribution is PMT = (FV - PV(1+r)^n) * r / ((1+r)^n - 1), where r is the monthly rate and n the number of months. At a 0% return, it is simply the amount needed divided by the number of months.

## Retirement savings targets
A common benchmark is to save about 15% of pre-tax income (including any employer match) for retirement across a career. Rough milestones some planners use are about 1x salary saved by 30, 3x by 40, 6x by 50, 8x by 60 and 10x by 67. The "4% rule" suggests a portfolio of about 25 times annual spending can support inflation-adjusted withdrawals for roughly 30 years, based on historical U.S. data; it is a guideline, not a guarantee.

## Home down payment
A 20% down payment avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI) on conventional loans, though many buyers put down less. A common affordability guideline is keeping total housing costs below about 28% of gross income and total debt payments below about 36%.

## Education savings
529 plans are tax-advantaged accounts for education. Earnings grow tax-free and withdrawals are tax-free for qualified education expenses. Many states offer a state income tax deduction for contributions.

## Accounting for inflation
Long-term goals should account for inflation. At 3% inflation, something costing $100,000 today costs about $134,000 in 10 years and $181,000 in 20 years.

## Reviewing and adjusting goals
Goals should be reviewed at least annually and after major life events such as a new job, marriage, a child, or a move. Automatic transfers on payday make consistent saving easier.

## Assumed returns in projections
Projections depend heavily on assumed returns. Conservative planning often uses about 5% to 7% nominal for a diversified stock-heavy portfolio, lower for balanced portfolios, and current savings rates for cash. Actual returns vary year to year and are not guaranteed.
